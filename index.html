01 / THE KEY DISTINCTION

Separate three concepts first

"Side channel" is a method of observing hidden state; "transient execution" is a mechanism that allows sensitive operations to occur temporarily. The two can be combined, but are not synonyms.

CHANNEL

Side channels

Secrets affect time, cache, power, or contention resources; observers infer secrets from statistical differences. Common cryptographic implementations may also have side channels, independent of speculative execution.

Start with timing and caching ↗
TRIGGER

Transient execution

Instructions executed during mispredictions, exceptions, or microcode assistance will not ultimately commit the architectural state, but may change the microarchitecture state.

Look at ROB, prediction and rollback ↗
ATTACK CHAIN

How they combine

Induces transient reading → encodes the value into an observable state → measures on the legal path → repeats, denoises and judges the signal.

02 / LEARNING ROUTE

Eight steps from observations to defenses

Existing knowledge of CPU architecture can condense step 1. Each step provides testable learning objectives rather than requiring you to read the entire paper from scratch.

01 · Architectural boundaries

Committed and uncommitted execution

Distinguish between ISA visible state and cache, predictor, buffer and other microarchitecture states.

Can explain why rollback is not equal to traceless ↗
02 · Measurement primitives

Timing and noise

Understand hit/miss distribution, repeated sampling, thresholds and error rates.

Be able to read a timing distribution graph ↗
03 · Cache side channel

Flush+Reload / Prime+Probe

Understand the shared memory conditions of the former and the cache set competition conditions of the latter.

Can draw the attacker and victim timeline ↗
04 · Speculation mechanism

Branches, faults, and data forwarding

Identifies mistraining, transient window, fault/assist and internal buffers.

Able to distinguish Spectre and Meltdown classes ↗
05 · Classic case

Spectre / Meltdown

Use V1, V2, and V3 as anchor points to mark triggers, cross-border boundaries, channels, and repair layers.

Be able to read the threat model from the announcement ↗
06 · New variant

MDS / GDS / BHI / SRSO

Put subsequent vulnerability back into the two types of maps: "Predictive Contamination" and "Data Sampling".

Be able to determine which category a new CVE belongs to ↗
07 · Controlled Experiment

Simulation → local timing → original PoC

First use the page model, then run the timing example of your own buffer, and finally read the researcher's source code and premise.

Able to write results and limitations ↗
08 · Defense review

Software, kernel, microcode, and platform

Review constant time, isolation, barriers, updates and residual risks based on the threat model.

Able to complete a one-page review checklist ↗
03 / ONE COMMON SHAPE

Ask five questions of every PoC

These five questions will help you read new papers and announcements more than memorizing attack names.

① Where is the secret?
② How to trigger?
③ Which boundary?
④ What traces are left?
⑤ How to measure?
Correct conclusion of the recurrence experiment:"No signal was observed under a certain configuration of this machine" only means that the measurement was unsuccessful and cannot independently prove that the CPU is not affected. Chip generation, microcode, kernel, compiler, virtualization, timing accuracy all affect the results.
