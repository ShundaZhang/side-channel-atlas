LVI · Load Value Injection

The victim’s own dependent instructions may briefly use a value that its load should not have supplied.

First understand the mechanism

Trigger
Victim load faults / assists / aborts
Data / prediction source
Wrong operand in victim dataflow
Architectural result
No result based on wrong operand
Affected transient behavior
Victim computes with wrong v

For teaching, use v = *config and result = v + 10. A normal v of 1 gives 11. Under an affected forwarding condition, a transient v of 0 could make the dependent operation compute 10, without committing that result. The 0 is an illustrative wrong operand, not the stolen secret, and +10 is not a disclosure gadget. A real attack needs additional victim behavior that exposes sensitive information.

Follow the teaching example

Numbered semantic pseudocode, not a runnable attack. Labels and values are illustrative; it does not configure predictors, manufacture faults or decode secrets. The diagram is conceptual, not a measured timing trace.
[01] victim: v = LOAD(config)
[02] victim: result = v + 10
[03] control: v = 1 → result = 11
[04] affected load condition may forward wrong v = 0
[05] transient: [02] computes 10
[06] architectural: do not commit result from the wrong v

Follow both [01] and [02] inside the victim. In [05] the victim computes with the wrong operand; the observer has not yet obtained a secret. If [04] forwards nothing, this particular dependent computation cannot use the illustrative zero early.

What is trained or triggered?

The core does not require branch training. Distinguish attacker-influenced stale-value forwarding, zero-only forwarding, and no-forwarding behavior. The availability and controllability of a wrong operand depend on the hardware and load condition.

Prerequisites and trust boundary

Original LVI work emphasizes SGX under an untrusted OS/VMM. Causing the required victim load condition and controlling the subsequent dataflow are substantial prerequisites. This is not a claim that arbitrary applications can inject persistent data into each other.

What does the mitigation interrupt?

Use the applicable SGX toolchain and platform guidance for load/dependency hardening. A generic instruction to clear MDS buffers is not a sufficient description of every LVI mitigation, especially when forwarding behaviors differ.

Do not confuse these concepts

Reverse the MDS viewpoint: the important event is a victim consuming the wrong value, not merely the observer reading a leftover sample.

Compare with MDS · RIDL / ZombieLoad / Fallout

How to read the original material

Read the LVI paper’s victim dataflow next to Intel’s forwarding categories. Label the injected operand and the eventual secret as different objects; do not infer a full exploit from this arithmetic model.
